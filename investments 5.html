How We Invest
A standard venture process, with diligence and scientific review at the center.
The steps will look familiar to any venture investor. What differs is where the weight sits: a reviewer-side read of the technology and its FDA pathway comes first, and scientific and regulatory diligence carry the most weight in the decision.
01
Sourcing and screening
Deals come from founders, angel groups, syndicates, and co-investors. A first screen checks stage, sector, ticket fit, and whether the product is a regulated healthcare technology.
Core of our process
02
Regulatory and scientific first read
A short reviewer-side read of the technology and its likely FDA pathway, before any deeper work. It decides whether a deal earns full diligence.
Core of our process
03
Full diligence
Scientific, regulatory, and commercial review run in parallel, with the most weight on the science and the pathway. The detail is set out below.
04
Decision and terms
Findings are written up plainly. Regulatory and scientific risk is priced into valuation and, where appropriate, tied to milestones in the terms.
05
Closing and onboarding
Documentation and closing, with a regulatory plan agreed with the founders from day one.
06
Support and follow-on
Regulatory support continues through each FDA milestone, and follow-on decisions are informed by real submission progress, not only a founder update.
Inside step 03
What full diligence covers.
Weighted most heavily
Scientific review
  • Whether the technology works as pitched: the mechanism, the performance claims, and the evidence behind them.
  • Data quality and integrity: how test data was generated, and whether the methods would stand up to FDA review.
  • Materials, biocompatibility, and chemistry for anything that contacts the body.
  • Whether the planned bench, analytical, and clinical evidence matches the claims being made.
  • Manufacturing and quality readiness: whether the product can be built consistently.
Regulatory review
  • FDA pathway and classification: 510(k), De Novo, PMA, or another route, and whether the claimed one is realistic.
  • Predicate strength and intended-use claims.
  • FDA interaction history: Form 483s, Warning Letters, deficiency responses, and pre-submission feedback.
  • Submission readiness, likely timeline, and the cost of reaching clearance or approval.
Commercial and financial review
  • Market, reimbursement, and adoption path.
  • Unit economics and use of proceeds, including the cost of regulatory and clinical work.
  • Team, cap table, intellectual property, and legal structure.
  • Run alongside co-investors where they are in the round, so the work is not duplicated.
