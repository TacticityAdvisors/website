A regulatory read before the term sheet, and a regulatory team after it.

01 · How We Back Founders
What you get beyond the check.
  • Regulatory pathway assessed before capital is committed

    Before any term sheet, we assess the likely FDA pathway, classification, and predicate landscape for the device. Founders get a straight answer on regulatory risk as part of diligence, not a surprise six months after the round closes.

  • Regulatory support from day one, through every FDA milestone

    Portfolio founders get direct access to regulatory guidance from the moment capital is deployed, not after a Series A when the product architecture is already locked in. Support continues through each submission, review cycle, and deficiency response.

  • Co-investment without requiring exclusivity

    We participate alongside angel groups, syndicates, and institutional investors, and bring regulatory diligence into the round for every investor at the table, not just ourselves.

  • Series A and beyond, through co-investment

    At later rounds, regulatory risk usually has the largest effect on valuation, and most generalist investors have the least ability to evaluate it. We invest within our usual range alongside VC and PE funds, adding regulatory-specific evaluation that complements their commercial and financial diligence.

  • Follow-on informed by real submission progress

    Because the same team that invests also evaluates regulatory milestones directly, follow-on decisions rest on a genuine read of submission progress, not just a founder update deck.

02 · Your Regulatory Route
Where your product sits, and what it means for the raise.
The FDA route shapes how much you need to raise, how long it takes, and what investors will ask. This is what we look at first for each type of product.
Your productTypical FDA routeWhat we look at first
A device with a comparable product already on the market510(k), based on substantial equivalence to a legally marketed predicateWhether the predicate is genuinely comparable, and whether the planned testing supports the equivalence claim.
A novel device of low to moderate risk with no suitable predicateDe Novo classification requestWhether the risk profile fits De Novo, and whether general or special controls can give reasonable assurance of safety and effectiveness.
A high-risk (Class III) devicePremarket approval (PMA)The clinical evidence, cost, and timeline the route demands, before they are built into the raise.
An in vitro diagnostic510(k), De Novo, or PMA, depending on riskAnalytical and clinical performance evidence, and the intended use claims.
Software or an AI-enabled product510(k), De Novo, or PMA, depending on risk and intended useThe intended use and risk category, and how software changes will be validated and controlled.
A drug or biologicIND, then NDA, ANDA, or BLAThe CMC package and manufacturing site readiness, including inspection readiness.

Where the route is unclear, a Q-Submission to FDA can confirm it before a company commits budget to a full submission.

03 · Questions We Ask Early
What to be ready to discuss.
These are the questions our first regulatory read starts from.
  • Intended use and claims

    What the product is for, who uses it, and the claims you plan to make. Claims drive the pathway.

  • Pathway and predicate

    The route you expect, the comparable products you rely on, and any feedback you have had from FDA.

  • Evidence so far and still to come

    Bench, animal, and clinical data to date, and the testing planned against which standards.

  • Materials and biocompatibility

    For anything that touches the body, what it is made of and what safety testing has been done.

  • Quality and manufacturing plan

    Design controls, data integrity practices, and how the product will be built consistently.

  • Time and budget to clearance

    Your own estimate of the time and cost of reaching clearance or approval, and how the raise covers it.
