Regulatory Reliance in Saudi Arabia: An Opportunity for Medical Device Manufacturers to Rethink Market Entry

November 23, 2026

How the SFDA’s September 2026 guidance allows manufacturers to leverage existing regulatory approvals when planning entry into the Saudi market.

At a Glance — Why Assess Reliance Eligibility?

Reduce duplicated assessment work by allowing the SFDA to consider relevant evaluations already completed by recognized authorities.

Make better use of existing approvals and supporting evidence, including MDSAP certificates and audit reports where accepted as QMS evidence.

Support launch planning with a defined review period for complete, eligible reliance applications.

Identify gaps early by checking device identity, approval basis and Saudi-specific requirements before submission.

Important: reliance is not automatic recognition. The SFDA retains decision-making authority and local requirements continue to apply.

01 Why Regulatory Reliance Matters

Obtaining approval in one market does not necessarily make entry into another straightforward. Medical device manufacturers may need to prepare similar technical documentation, submit safety and performance evidence, and undergo assessments that overlap with work already completed elsewhere.

For a manufacturer that already holds approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or conformity assessment under the European framework, repeating substantial parts of the process can add time, cost and administrative effort.

On 9 September 2026, the Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) published its Reliance Approach for Medical Devices (MDS-G-030). It allows eligible manufacturers to use existing regulatory approvals and assessment evidence from recognized authorities to support a Saudi Medical Device Marketing Authorization (MDMA) application.

02 What Regulatory Reliance Means

Regulatory reliance allows an authority to take account of an assessment already conducted by another trusted regulator, rather than repeating every part of the review. For medical devices, this may include existing marketing authorizations, conformity assessments, quality audits and other regulatory evidence.

It is not automatic recognition. The receiving authority remains responsible for deciding whether the device can be marketed locally and whether the evidence is sufficient. National requirements continue to apply.

The IMDRF Playbook for Medical Device Regulatory Reliance Programs (IMDRF/GRRP WG/N89), published in February 2026, provides a framework for regulators seeking to reduce unnecessary duplication while maintaining appropriate oversight.

03 Which Reference Jurisdictions Does the SFDA Recognize?

MDS-G-030 identifies six reference jurisdictions whose regulatory decisions and assessment evidence may be considered under the reliance approach:

JurisdictionReference Authority
United StatesUS Food and Drug Administration (FDA)
European Union / EEARelevant EEA competent authorities
United KingdomMedicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)
CanadaHealth Canada
AustraliaTherapeutic Goods Administration (TGA)
JapanPharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency (PMDA)
What This Means in Practice

An approval from one of these jurisdictions is a starting point for the assessment, not proof that the product automatically qualifies. Manufacturers still need to show that the specific device and its approval route meet the SFDA’s eligibility criteria.

04 Start With the Device: Does It Qualify?

The SFDA expects the device to have identical technical aspects to the device authorized by the reference authority. Manufacturers should be able to demonstrate consistency in the following areas:

  • Design: The device configuration covered by the reference authorization.
  • Manufacturing process: The manufacturing arrangements relevant to the authorized device.
  • Intended use: The intended purpose covered by the reference authorization.

The manufacturer must also provide evidence that the reference authorization remains valid. The brand name alone is not enough: the manufacturer needs to establish that the device submitted to the SFDA is the same device covered by the reference approval.

A Useful Eligibility Question

Do not ask only, “Do we already have an approval?” Ask instead, “Is this exact device, under this approval route and with this supporting evidence, eligible under the SFDA’s reliance criteria?”

05 Exclusions: Where Manufacturers Need to Pause

MDS-G-030 does not make the reliance pathway available to every medical device or every type of regulatory approval. Review these exclusions before committing to the pathway:

  • Predicate-equivalence approvals: Products authorized primarily through substantial equivalence to a predicate device are excluded. This is particularly relevant to US FDA 510(k) clearances. A 510(k) should not be assumed to qualify automatically; assess the actual approval basis against the SFDA guidance.
  • Self-registration or self-declaration: Routes without independent assessment by a regulatory authority or recognized third-party conformity assessment body are excluded.
  • Custom-made devices: Devices that meet the applicable custom-made definition are excluded.
  • Significant post-market safety concerns: Products associated with significant recalls, suspensions, withdrawals or other regulatory restrictions by a reference authority may be excluded.
  • Certain SaMD and AI-enabled devices: Eligibility may depend on intended use, risk, novelty, complexity and other SFDA considerations.
Do Not Treat This as a Tick-Box Exercise

An approval from a recognized jurisdiction does not override an exclusion. Check the basis of the original decision and the product’s regulatory history before selecting the reliance route.

06 What Evidence Can Manufacturers Reuse?

Manufacturers may already hold technical information, quality records, risk management documentation, performance evidence and product information from earlier submissions. The practical task is to identify what can support the Saudi application and what needs to be updated or supplemented.

The SFDA recognizes MDSAP certificates and audit reports issued by recognized MDSAP Auditing Organizations as evidence of conformity with quality management system requirements. This can help manufacturers avoid duplicating QMS assessment activities.

Important Distinction

MDSAP evidence supports the quality management system element. It is not an exemption from the full Saudi MDMA application or other applicable local requirements.

07 How the Application Process Works

The reliance pathway does not remove the need for an MDMA application. It changes how the SFDA can use existing regulatory evidence during review.

StepManufacturer’s Action
1. Check eligibilityReview the reference approval, approval basis, device identity and relevant exclusions.
2. Confirm classificationDetermine the applicable SFDA risk classification.
3. Prepare the evidenceCompile valid reference-approval evidence, the completed Reliance Form and required supporting documents.
4. SubmitApply through SFDA E-Services and address establishment and local requirements.
5. Respond to questionsBe prepared to provide further information if requested by the SFDA.

The SFDA may request further information depending on the device’s novelty, complexity and risk profile. A reliance application is therefore not simply a documentation-transfer exercise.

08 What Does the 35–60 Working-Day Timeline Mean?

SFDA Reliance Review Timeline

35–60 working days: the specified review period for complete reliance applications across Classes A, B, C and D.

Potentially 35 working days: where the SFDA relies on the prior reference-authority approval instead of conducting a full independent assessment, subject to the applicable conditions.

This is a review timeline, not a guaranteed approval date. It applies to complete applications and remains subject to eligibility and any additional information requests.

For manufacturers, regulatory timelines affect launch planning, distributor arrangements, commercial commitments and the sequence of international market entry. The timeline should therefore be treated as a planning input, not as a confirmed market authorization date.

09 Reliance After Market Authorization

The guidance also addresses the use of information from reference authorities for post-market surveillance and regulatory oversight. Safety signals, recalls, field safety corrective actions and other regulatory developments in one market may have implications for the same device in another.

Reliance may help the SFDA make use of information already available from recognized authorities, but it does not transfer the manufacturer’s responsibility for device safety or remove its Saudi post-market obligations.

10 Does Reliance Extend to Therapeutics and Combination Products?

The pathway discussed in this article is specific to medical devices under MDS-G-030. The SFDA Drug Sector has a separate Verification and Abridged Pathways guidance (DS-G-020, Version 3.0) for eligible new chemical entities and biological medicines for human and veterinary use. The verification pathway uses approvals from both the FDA and EMA, while the abridged pathway may rely on approval from either authority. Certain categories, including blood products, advanced therapy medicinal products and veterinary vaccines, are excluded.

Combination products require a product-specific assessment. The SFDA has separate guidance on their classification and registration, but this should not be read as confirmation that every combination product qualifies for a reliance pathway. Manufacturers should first establish the product’s SFDA classification and responsible sector, then check the applicable sector-specific requirements.

11 A Practical Pre-Submission Check

Before preparing a reliance application, manufacturers should be able to answer these questions:

Area to CheckQuestion to Resolve
Reference approvalIs there a valid authorization from one of the six recognized jurisdictions?
Approval basisWas the product independently assessed, or does its route fall within an exclusion?
Device consistencyDo design, manufacturing process and intended use match the reference-authorized device?
DocumentationWhich existing records can be reused, and what needs to be supplemented?
Saudi requirementsAre local labeling, IFU, language, electrical and storage requirements addressed?
Post-market readinessAre safety monitoring and post-market arrangements appropriate for Saudi Arabia?

12 What This Means for Manufacturers

MDS-G-030 gives medical device manufacturers a reason to review their existing regulatory portfolios before preparing a new Saudi submission. The opportunity is to make better use of work already completed, while recognizing that device identity, approval basis, supporting evidence and Saudi requirements remain central to eligibility.
